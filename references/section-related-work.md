# Related Work Section Guide

Use this reference when drafting or revising related work.

## Goal

Position the paper against the most relevant research lines and make novelty easy to verify. Related work should help reviewers understand the relationship between prior methods and the present contribution, not merely prove that the authors have read many papers.

## Workflow

1. List directly competing and recent baseline papers first.
2. Group literature by technical topic, assumption, or failure mode.
3. For each topic, summarize the common paradigm.
4. Explain the limitation or open gap relevant to this paper.
5. Clarify how the present work differs.

## Topic Design

Use two to four focused topics, such as:

- Mainstream methods for the target task.
- Closest methods to the core idea.
- Auxiliary techniques used by the method.
- New task, dataset, metric, or evaluation setting.

## Paragraph Template

1. Topic sentence defining the scope.
2. Representative methods and their shared pattern.
3. Limitation tied to the paper's target challenge.
4. Relationship to the present method.

## Do And Do Not

- Compare mechanisms, assumptions, data regimes, objectives, and failure modes.
- Cover the strongest recent competitors.
- Use respectful contrast language.
- Do not turn related work into a citation dump.
- Do not hide close prior work.
- Do not claim that no work exists unless a careful search supports it.

## Output Pattern

Return:

1. Proposed topic clusters.
2. Missing or likely-needed citation categories.
3. Revised related-work paragraphs.
4. A difference table if several close methods are easy to confuse.
