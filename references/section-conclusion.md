# Conclusion Section Guide

Use this reference when drafting or revising a conclusion, limitations paragraph, or future-work paragraph.

## Goal

Close with a precise takeaway, credible evidence summary, honest limitations, and a future direction that follows from the paper's scope.

## Structure

1. Restate the solved problem and core technical idea.
2. Summarize the strongest evidence.
3. State the practical value, new insight, or capability enabled.
4. Acknowledge limitations as scope boundaries.
5. End with a concrete future direction.

## Limitation Guidance

Prefer limitations tied to the task setting:

- Data regime or distribution boundary.
- Assumption about sensors, views, labels, prompts, domains, or supervision.
- Deployment scope or compute requirement.
- Generalization to harder scenes, languages, domains, or time horizons.

Distinguish a scope limitation from a technical defect. If the method underperforms strong baselines on a central metric, do not hide it as future work; narrow the claim and explain the tradeoff.

## Writing Rules

- Do not introduce a new major claim in the conclusion.
- Do not repeat all contribution bullets mechanically.
- Keep evidence summary specific but short.
- Avoid vague endings such as "we believe this will inspire future work" unless the future direction is concrete.
- Make limitations consistent with the results and ethics sections.

## Output Pattern

Return:

1. Conclusion outline.
2. Revised conclusion.
3. Limitation and future-work alternatives.
4. Claim-evidence check for any strong final claim.
